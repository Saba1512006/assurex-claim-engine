How a claim is decided

Five steps, every one recorded

The same claim record goes to both models in different formats. Neither model sees the other's answer, and the Claim Summary Card never contains a prediction or decision.

Collect

Product, warranty, fault details and evidence. Receipts are read by OCR and confirmed by the user.

Check

Dates, serial numbers, duplicates and missing documents are compared across every source.

Predict twice

Python model on the structured record; Teachable Machine on the Claim Summary Card image.

Compare

Class match and |confidence difference| give one of five consistency statuses.

Decide

A config-driven decision table combines rules and models: Likely Valid, Likely Invalid or Manual Review Required.

Python classification model

Three algorithms compared with 5-fold cross-validation, selected on the validation split, then probability-calibrated so confidence scores mean something.

Google Teachable Machine

Trained on 2,100 square Claim Summary Cards (two variations per training claim). Validation and test cards never leave the evaluation set.

Readable warranty rules

Coverage, grace periods, reporting deadlines, exclusions and document rules live in one JSON file per category, editable by administrators.

Duplicate & fraud checks

SHA-256 hashes of every file, invoice-number reuse, open claims on the same serial and near-identical fault descriptions.

Human in the loop

Reviewers see both predictions, every rule, contradictions and evidence. Overrides need a written reason and stay in the audit trail.

Access by role and record

Customers see their own claims, staff their service center's, reviewers the queue. Everything else returns “not found”.
